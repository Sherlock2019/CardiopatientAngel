# Analytics methods

All analytics are **descriptive** and computed on **synthetic** data. They show where alarm burden was
observed; they do not estimate causal effects, predict clinical outcomes, or validate any clinical finding.

## Exposure (monitored patient-time)

- A patient's monitoring window is `admission_date → monitoring_end` (or *now* if still monitored), clipped to the analysis date range.
- **Rate = alarms ÷ exposure × 100** → *alarms per 100 monitored patient-days* (heatmap & shifts use patient-hours).
- Coverage gaps inside the window (missing readings) do not reduce exposure: the monitoring order was in effect.

## Time-valid cohort attribution

Diagnoses, medications and treatments are intervals. For a cohort *X*:
- exposure = time the patient was monitored **and** *X* was valid (diagnosis date → resolution; medication start → stop; treatment start → end), with overlapping orders of the same class merged so time is never double counted;
- alarms = alarms that fired while *X* was valid.

So a heart-failure diagnosis made mid-stay contributes only post-diagnosis time and alarms, and a diagnosis resolved
before admission contributes nothing. **Patients belong to several cohorts at once**, so diagnosis/medication/treatment
cohort counts overlap and do not sum to the total.

**Cohort Explorer**: OR within a filter group, AND across groups. Exposure is the intersection of the selected
intervals. An hour-of-day filter keeps only alarms in those hours and scales exposure by `hours selected ÷ 24`
(an approximation that assumes even census over the day).

## Other definitions

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Repeat alarm | same patient, same alarm type, within the repeat window (default 120 min, configurable) of the previous one |
| Acknowledgment time | `acknowledged_at − triggered_at` (median and 90th percentile) |
| % patients with ≥1 alarm | patients with an attributed alarm ÷ patients with exposure in the cohort |
| Hour/day of week | computed in the selected timezone (default hospital timezone `HOSPITAL_TZ`) |
| Shifts | Day 07–15, Evening 15–23, Night 23–07 (local) |
| Small cohorts | < 20 patients flagged; < 10 patients: rates suppressed (both configurable) |

## Operational Attention Indicator — Demo Only

Additive, transparent points per patient with an open alarm (weights configurable in Settings):
highest unresolved source severity (critical 40 / warning 20 / advisory 5) + 5 per alarm in the last 6 h (cap 25)
+ 1 per minute the oldest alarm is unacknowledged (cap 20) + 5 per repeat alarm (cap 15); capped at 100.
It is **not** a clinical risk score and is never used to rank clinical urgency. The queue is ordered by the
alarm's **source severity**, then unacknowledged first, then oldest.

## Outcome comparison (deceased vs. discharged alive)

Completed stays whose monitoring ended in the analysis window. For each dimension (age group, disease, treatment,
medication class, care unit, sex, critical alarms during stay), the two tables show how each group is composed
(% of deceased vs. % of survivors in each category). The mortality panel shows the in-hospital mortality per category
with Wilson 95% confidence intervals. "Where the groups differ most" ranks categories by the percentage-point gap.

**Why this cannot tell you what prevents death:** confounding by indication (sicker patients get more treatment),
survival-time bias (exposures that start later in a stay, such as rehabilitation, can only happen to patients who
survive long enough), overlapping categories, small groups, and synthetic data generated by known rules. A real
analysis would need a pre-registered question, adjustment for severity (e.g., regression or matching), time-varying
exposure methods, and clinical/statistical review.

## Injected synthetic patterns (the "findings" the demo will show)

Alarm generation (`app/seed.py` → `INJECTED_PATTERNS`):
- hazard rises with age group (×0.7 under 50 → ×1.8 for 90+);
- heart failure, tachyarrhythmia, AF and bradycardia add hazard; CCU and Post-Op higher than telemetry wards;
- hourly peak 06:00–09:00 local, secondary 18:00–20:00; slower acknowledgment 06:00–09:00 and at night;
- antiarrhythmics → more HR-low / irregular-rhythm alarms; diuretics → more BP-low; wearable patches → more signal loss;
- ~30% of alarms followed by 1–3 same-type repeats within 5–90 min; per-patient frailty (gamma) heterogeneity.

Outcomes (`OUTCOME_PATTERNS`): mortality rises with age, heart failure (×2.0), tachyarrhythmia, AF, CCU stay and
critical-alarm count; cardiac rehabilitation ×0.45 (confounded by design), anticoagulants in AF ×0.7, beta blockers in
HF ×0.75.

Data realism: uneven coverage (65–100% of scheduled readings), 3% missing, 1.5% artifacts, 4% delayed (5–45 min),
2% duplicated deliveries (dropped by the idempotency key), multiple diagnoses/medications per patient, historical
resolved diagnoses. Fixed random seed (`SEED_RANDOM=42`); set `SEED_ANCHOR` (ISO time) for a byte-identical dataset.
